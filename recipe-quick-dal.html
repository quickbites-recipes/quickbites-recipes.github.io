Quick Dal (Lentil Soup)

Not every dal needs a pressure cooker and an hour of simmering. Red lentils (masoor dal) break down fast on their own, which means a proper, comforting dal is well within reach on a weeknight.

Ingredients

Serves 2-3.

  • Red lentils (masoor dal), rinsed1 cup
  • Water3 cups
  • Turmeric powder1/2 tsp
  • Saltto taste
  • Ghee or oil2 tbsp
  • Cumin seeds1 tsp
  • Garlic, sliced4 cloves
  • Dried red chilies2

Method

  1. Cook the lentils. Add the rinsed lentils, water, turmeric, and salt to a pot. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer and cook uncovered for 12-15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils fall apart and the dal thickens.
  2. Adjust consistency. If the dal looks too thick, stir in a little hot water to loosen it to a soup-like consistency.
  3. Make the tadka (tempering). In a small pan, heat the ghee over medium heat. Add the cumin seeds and let them sizzle for a few seconds, then add the garlic and dried chilies. Cook until the garlic turns golden brown — watch closely, as it can burn quickly.
  4. Combine. Pour the hot tempering directly over the cooked dal — it should sizzle on contact. Stir once and serve immediately with rice or roti.

Tip: Red lentils don't need soaking, which is exactly why they're the fastest dal to cook — save the soaking-required lentils for a weekend.

Why this works

The tempering (tadka) is what separates a flat-tasting dal from a fragrant one. Cooking whole spices and garlic in hot fat, then pouring it over the finished dal at the last second, keeps the aroma sharp instead of dulling it by cooking the spices in with everything else.
